    Miraz 2024 — Design Works

    Led a 28-volunteer design team to give a 72-event fest one coherent visual identity — backed by a shared Figma design system that turned one-off requests into reusable, fast-to-ship assets.

    Role: Design Head Team: 28 volunteers Timeline: Jan – May 2024 Type: Design & Brand Systems

    Situation

    A fest encompassing three fests within itself - Cultural, Sports, and Tech events. Carrying IIT Mandi's fest tradition of overarching themes forward, this time with Retro/Arcade being the theme.

    72 events, one recognisable visual language, not 72 one-off designs.

    With dozens of volunteers producing posters, merchandise, promotional content, and fest websites, the risk was a scattered identity and a lot of duplicated work.


    The design team had to make the fest look like one coherent brand while still shipping everything on a student timeline.

    Context & Constraints

    • The Team: 28 volunteers with different skill levels and availability.
    • Volume: posters for 72 events, plus artwork, merchandise, promotional content, and fest websites.
    • Consistency: every touchpoint had to deliver the same unified-fest identity.
    • Speed: a short Jan–May window, reusable systems rather than perfect one-offs.

    My Role

    I was Design Head — responsible for the visual direction and for making the team's output consistent. Concretely, that meant:

    • Defining the unified visual theme for the fest.
    • Setting up and maintaining the shared Figma design system.
    • Coordinating 28 volunteers across posters, merchandise, promotion, and web.
    • Reviewing work so the identity held together at scale.

    Approach

    The work came down to four decisions:

    1. Theme first, assets second.
    Before producing individual pieces, I locked the unified visual theme so every later asset had a single source of truth to follow.

    2. A shared Figma system.
    I built reusable components, styles, and prototypes so volunteers could assemble work quickly instead of redesigning from scratch each time.

    3. Design for the whole fest.
    The system had to stretch across very different surfaces — cultural events, sports events, and tech events, resulting in a total of 72 event posters, 10+ artworks, 2 merchandise items, promotional content, and the fest websites.

    4. Reuse over reinvention.
    One-off event posters became templates, a unified theme brought a consistent design language across the materials.

    Outcome

    • 90+ Figma assets delivered across the fest.
    • Posters for 72 events, all under one visual theme.
    • Artwork, merchandise, promotional content, and fest websites built from the same identity.
    • Reusable components, styles, and prototypes — faster delivery and consistent brand identity across all assets.
    • A team of 28 volunteers working from one shared design system.

    Reflection

    • Systems beat heroics. The reusable Figma components mattered more than any single poster — they multiplied the whole team's output.
    • Consistency is an operations problem. Keeping 28 people visually aligned is less about taste and more about giving everyone the same building blocks.
    • Design thinking is product thinking. Defining constraints, standardising the workflow, and shipping under a deadline are the same muscles I bring to product work.
